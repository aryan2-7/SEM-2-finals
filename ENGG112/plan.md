# ENGG 112 — 5-Day Study Plan
## Sunday 23 Aug → Wednesday 26 Aug (exam: Thursday 27 Aug)

**Budget:** 4–6 hrs/day, ENGG 112 only. Total ~20–30 hrs across 5 days.
**Strategy:** Front-load the highest-yield topics (see priority list), build volume evenly across all 4 chapters, end with full timed practice papers.

Each day is split into three blocks. Take a real break between blocks — don't chain them.

---

## Day 1 — Sunday 23 Aug: Chapter 1 Foundations
**Goal:** Rebuild DC circuit theorems from scratch, fast and cold.

**Block 1 (~2 hrs) — Reduction & Theorems**
- Resistance reduction: delta-star, bridge circuits, cube networks (2022, 2024 Section A style)
- Source conversion (voltage ↔ current sources)
- Do 5–6 MCQ-style reduction problems from the papers, timed (aim <2 min each)

**Block 2 (~2 hrs) — Mesh & Nodal Analysis**
- Redo mesh analysis on 3 different circuits from the papers (2018 Fig.2, 2022 Fig.2, 2024 Fig.2)
- Redo nodal analysis on 3 different circuits (2024 Fig.1, 2025 Fig.3)
- Focus on setting up equations fast — this is the time sink in the real exam

**Block 3 (~1–2 hrs) — Thevenin/Norton + Superposition**
- Pick 2 Thevenin problems (2022, 2025) and solve fully
- Pick 2 superposition problems and solve fully
- End of day: attempt the full **2025 March/April Section A** (MCQ) cold, timed 15 min, check answers

**End-of-day checkpoint:** Can you do a 4-resistor mesh analysis in under 8 minutes without looking anything up?

---

## Day 2 — Monday 24 Aug: Chapter 2 — AC Fundamentals Part 1
**Goal:** Waveforms + impedance/phasors, since RMS/average is your most reliable guaranteed marks.

**Block 1 (~2 hrs) — RMS/Average of Waveforms**
- This is non-negotiable — do EVERY waveform problem from every paper you have (2018 Fig.5, 2019 Fig.7, 2022 Fig.6, 2024 Fig.5, Fig.8, 2025 Fig.5)
- Build a mental checklist: identify shape → find period → integrate piecewise → don't forget to square before averaging for RMS

**Block 2 (~2 hrs) — Impedance, Phasors, Phase Angles**
- Rectangular ↔ polar conversion drills (do 10 quick ones)
- Phasor addition of two sinusoids (2022, 2024, 2025 style — practice UNUSUAL phase differences, not just 90°)
- Impedance in parallel/series with complex numbers (2018 Fig.6, 2024 Fig.6)

**Block 3 (~1–2 hrs) — Power Triangle**
- Active/reactive/apparent power problems (every paper has one)
- Power factor from impedance and vice versa
- Mixed practice: 8–10 quick power-triangle MCQs from Section A across papers

**End-of-day checkpoint:** Given a random waveform, can you find RMS in under 6 minutes?

---

## Day 3 — Tuesday 25 Aug: Chapter 2 Part 2 + Chapter 3
**Goal:** Resonance (both directions) + transformers/magnetic circuits.

**Block 1 (~2 hrs) — Series RLC Resonance**
- Memorize formulas cold first: f₀, Q, BW, half-power frequencies, X_L=X_C condition
- Forward direction: given R,L,C → find everything (2018, 2025 style)
- **Backward direction: given BW and Q → find R,L,C** (2024 style — don't skip this, it's the trap)

**Block 2 (~1.5 hrs) — Parallel RLC / Nodal AC**
- Practical parallel resonance derivation (2019, 2024)
- One AC nodal analysis problem with complex impedances (2019 Fig.7 j10Ω branch, 2024 Fig.6)

**Block 3 (~2 hrs) — Transformers & Magnetic Circuits**
- Write out the EMF equation derivation once, cleanly, from memory — then check it
- Turns ratio / induced voltage / max flux numericals (every paper has one — do 3)
- Magnetic circuit MMF/reluctance/flux problems, including ones with air gaps (2019, 2024, 2025 — air gaps are the part people get wrong)

**End-of-day checkpoint:** Can you derive the transformer EMF equation without notes?

---

## Day 4 — Wednesday 26 Aug: Chapter 4 + DC Generator + Weak-Spot Sweep
**Goal:** 3-phase systems, DC generator theory, and patch any remaining gaps.

**Block 1 (~2 hrs) — 3-Phase Star/Delta**
- This is usually the biggest Section B question (6–11 marks) — treat it as such
- Do at least 3 full problems: star-star (2018, 2024), star-delta (2024 Fig.9, 2025 Fig.8)
- Drill the 120° phase angle relationships and line vs. phase V/I relations until automatic
- Practice both √3·V_L·I_L·cosφ and 3·V_ph·I_ph·cosφ — know when each applies

**Block 2 (~1 hr) — DC Generator Theory**
- Write your "construction and working principle" answer once, in full, exam-ready
- Sketch the labeled diagram from memory
- Memorize it — this is close to a free 5 marks every year

**Block 3 (~2–3 hrs) — Weak Spot Sweep**
- Go back through Section A across ALL 7 papers and drill anything you got wrong or hesitated on: resistor color codes, form factor, commutator function, delta-star numbers, reactance-frequency scaling
- Redo one problem from each of Days 1–3 that felt shaky

**End-of-day checkpoint:** No topic on the priority list should still feel unfamiliar.

---

## Day 5 — Thursday 27 Aug (exam day, morning): Timed Full Papers
**Goal:** Simulate real exam conditions, not new learning.

- Pick your **weakest** full past paper (by feel) and do it timed, Section A in the actual time limit (30 min) and Section B in 2.5 hrs if you have the runway — otherwise compress to a 1-hour version hitting one question per chapter
- If time is tight before the exam, instead do a rapid-fire pass: one Section A set (15 min) + the 3-phase question + the waveform question, since those are your highest-yield, highest-effort-to-marks ratio items
- Skim your DC generator answer and EMF derivation one more time
- Don't start anything new — consolidate only

---

## General Rules Across All 5 Days
- **Every day starts with a 10-minute formula recall** (write from memory: resonance formulas, power formulas, transformer EMF equation, 3-phase relations) before opening any notes
- **Timed practice > passive reading** — always solve before checking the paper's likely approach
- Take breaks between blocks — 4-6 hrs is meant to include rest, not be 6 hrs straight
- If a topic block runs short, don't skip ahead — use the extra time on more Section A drills from that chapter instead
