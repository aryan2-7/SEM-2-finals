## Top 7 Highest-Yield Topics

### 1. RMS/Average of Periodic Waveforms
Appeared in literally every paper, always Section B, always a slightly different weird waveform (sawtooth, pulse train, half-wave). This is the single most reliable 5-marker.
- Practice integrating at least 5 different waveform shapes
- Memorize the standard forms (square, triangular, sawtooth) so you don't re-derive from scratch

### 2. Mesh + Nodal Analysis (DC and AC)
Guaranteed every year, often two separate questions in Section B worth 5–6 marks each. Sometimes with complex impedances (AC).
- Drill both DC versions and one with j-notation impedances
- Practice setting up matrix equations fast — this is where time is lost in exams

### 3. Thevenin's / Norton's + Superposition
Combined they show up in nearly every paper, sometimes as the same circuit asked two ways.
- Practice finding Thevenin resistance by source suppression
- Do at least 3 problems with multi-source circuits

### 4. 3-Phase Star/Delta Systems
Almost always the anchor question of Section B (6–11 marks) — phase angle finding, line/phase relations, power, sometimes with unbalanced or delta-connected loads.
- Master phase angle relationships (120° apart) cold
- Practice both star-star and star-delta configurations

### 5. Series RLC Resonance
Resonant frequency, Q-factor, bandwidth, half-power frequencies — shows up as both MCQ concepts and full numericals every year.
- Memorize all formulas: f₀, Q, BW, half-power points
- Practice going from given BW+Q backward to find R, L, C (2024 paper style)

### 6. Transformer EMF + Magnetic Circuits
Guaranteed derivation (EMF equation, 2–3 marks) plus a numerical on turns ratio/flux/MMF, and often a separate magnetic circuit reluctance problem.
- Know the EMF equation derivation by heart
- Practice reluctance/MMF problems with air gaps — they trip people up

### 7. DC Generator Theory
Nearly guaranteed 5-mark theory question every single year — construction and working principle.
- Write out a clean, complete answer once and just memorize the structure
- Include a labeled diagram if you can draw one quickly

## Section A (MCQ) Recurring Favorites
Cheap, fast points if you drill the concept list:
- Color coding of resistors
- Delta-star equivalence numbers
- Maximum power transfer condition
- Form factor
- Three-phase power formula variants (P = √3·V_L·I_L·cosφ vs. P = 3·V_ph·I_ph·cosφ — they love testing whether you know which is line and which is phase)
- Reactance vs. frequency scaling
- Commutator function

## Notes on Exam Format & Recent Trends

- **Papers get shorter over time.** 2018/2019 had 20 marks in Section A + 55 in Section B. By 2022 onward it's 10 marks in A + 40 in B. Recent papers (2022, 2024, 2025) are the better format-match for what you'll face — weight your practice toward those three.
- **2024 and 2025 added twists** on standard problems:
  - Working *backward* from bandwidth/Q to find R, L, C values (rather than the usual forward direction)
  - Phase-relationship-between-two-sinusoids questions with slightly unusual phase differences
  - If you've only practiced the "given R, L, C find resonance" direction, these can catch you off guard — make sure to practice both directions.
