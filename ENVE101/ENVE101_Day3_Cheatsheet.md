# Day 3 Cheat Sheet — Full Drilling + Weak Spots (Exam Day or Day Before)

Goal today isn't new material — it's **finding out what you actually don't know** and fixing exactly that, then rehearsing pacing under real time pressure. ~3.5–4 hours, front-loaded early in the day.

---

## Block 1 — MCQ Speed Run (2 hrs)

### How to run it
- Go through **Section A of all 6 past papers**, back to back, timed.
- Give yourself **~45-60 seconds per question** max — that's the real exam pace (30 min ÷ 20 Q).
- **Rule:** if you don't know it in that window, don't sit and derive it. **Flag it and move on.** The point of this block is diagnosis, not mastery yet.
- Don't check answers as you go — do a full pass first, then grade in one batch. Interrupting your rhythm to check each answer teaches you nothing about real exam pacing.

### After the pass — Tally your flags
Sort your flagged/wrong questions into buckets. This tells you where Block 2 time actually goes (don't guess — use the real tally):

| Bucket | If flags cluster here |
|---|---|
| Mass balance / CSTR setup | Revisit Day 1 §1 |
| Population projection | Revisit Day 1 §2 |
| Water balance | Revisit Day 1 §3 |
| Solid waste (MC%, density, landfill) | Revisit Day 1 §4 |
| ESP / cyclone / dB | Revisit Day 2 §1-3 |
| Air pollutants / AQI / AQS / Nepal standards | Revisit Day 2 §4 |
| Water treatment / sewers / HRT | Revisit Day 2 §5 |
| LCA / Functional Unit / EIA-IEE-BES | Revisit Day 2 §6 |
| Green Engineering | Revisit Day 2 §7 |
| Ch.1 (IRR, NEC, design types) | Revisit Day 2 §8 |

**Be honest with the tally.** If 6 of your 8 flags are unit-conversion slips in solid waste problems, that's your actual weak spot — not "I don't know solid waste," but specifically "I rush the density formula." Fix the precise thing.

---

## Block 2 — Targeted Fix + Timed Mock (1.5 hrs)

### Part A: Fix weak spots directly (45 min)
- Pull up the exact cheat-sheet section for each weak bucket.
- Re-derive/re-solve 1-2 of the flagged questions **from scratch**, writing every step out — don't just re-read the answer.
- If a whole topic bucket is weak (not just one slip), do one more fresh practice problem from a past paper you haven't used yet for that topic.

### Part B: One timed Section B mock (45 min)
- Pick the past paper you've used **least** so far.
- Set a timer for the actual proportional time (Section B is worth ~40-48 marks out of the 2hr30min block — roughly **100-110 minutes** for Section B+C combined in the real exam; do a **45-min slice** covering 3-4 questions to check pacing, not the whole section).
- Write full answers, including diagrams where asked (mass balance diagrams, treatment schematics) — diagrams are often worth marks on their own and are easy to forget under time pressure.
- **After:** check how many you finished in time vs. rushed vs. left blank. That's your real pacing signal for tomorrow.

---

## Final Pass — Morning of Exam (30 min, do NOT skip, do NOT extend)

- [ ] Re-read your **formula sheet** (Day 1 cheat sheet, section-by-section) top to bottom once. Don't re-solve, just re-read to refresh recall.
- [ ] Re-read your **4 rehearsed Green Engineering answers** once, out loud if possible — verbal recall is a different retrieval path than reading and catches gaps reading alone misses.
- [ ] Skim the **quick-recall lists**: 6 criteria pollutants, sewer size order, LCA 4 stages, waste hierarchy, BES/IEE/EIA thresholds.
- [ ] **Do NOT start new material this close to the exam.** At this point new information has near-zero retention value and mainly adds anxiety. If you spot a genuine gap, note it and let it go — better to walk in calm and solid on 90% than shaky trying to cram the last 10%.

---

## Exam-Day Execution Notes (worth reading once, cheap insurance)

- **Section A first, fast:** you drilled this — don't second-guess yourself into overtime. If unsure, mark your best guess and move on; don't burn Section B time on a 1-mark question.
- **Section B — grab the easy numericals first:** population projection, water balance, and solid waste table problems are the most formulaic — bank those marks early before tackling anything that needs more thinking (LCA scenario questions, "explain the role of" essays).
- **Always draw the mass balance diagram when asked** — it's explicitly marked in several past papers ("draw a mass balance diagram for the above system [1]") and takes 30 seconds.
- **Show units at every step** — several past papers have marks split like [2] for setup + [1] for units/final answer; losing the unit mark on an otherwise-correct calc is the easiest mark to leave on the table.
- **If a Section C "differentiate between" or "short notes" question gives you an OR / choose-2-of-N**, pick the ones you rehearsed on Day 2 (Cyclone vs ESP, IEE vs EIA) rather than improvising on the spot.
