# ENVE 101 — 3-Day Study Plan (Exam in 2–3 Days)

Starting point: attended lectures, no review done yet.
Strategy: skip passive re-reading. Go straight to formulas + worked examples + past-paper MCQs, since that's exactly what's tested. Chapter reading is only for the "explain/differentiate" written questions.

**Total estimated study time: ~14–16 hours** across 3 days. If you only have 2 days, see the compression note at the bottom.

---

## Time Estimate by Topic

| Topic | Time needed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mass balance (general eq. + CSTR numericals) | 2.5 hrs | Conceptually dense, appears every paper, needs derivation practice not just formula memorization |
| Population projection (arithmetic/geometric) | 1 hr | Formulaic once you've done it twice |
| Water balance (evapotranspiration) | 1 hr | Same formula every time, just plug-in practice |
| Solid waste (MC%, density table, landfill area) | 2 hrs | Table-based calc takes practice to do fast + Modified Dulong's formula |
| Air pollution (ESP, cyclone, dB, criteria pollutants) | 2 hrs | ESP numerical needs practice; rest is recall |
| Water/wastewater treatment + sewers | 1.5 hrs | Mostly recall — process train, sewer hierarchy, BOD mixing |
| LCA + Functional Unit + EIA/IEE/BES | 1.5 hrs | Written-answer material, needs 2-3 examples memorized |
| Green Engineering principles | 45 min | Just need to explain any 2 well |
| Ch.1 intro topics (IRR, NEC, design types) | 45 min | Low density, quick recall |
| MCQ speed drilling (all past papers, Section A only) | 2 hrs | Best marks-per-minute activity — do this last |
| Buffer / weak-spot review | 1.5 hrs | Whatever trips you up in drilling |

## Day 1 (today) — Foundations + Numericals Core
**~5.5 hours**

### Morning/Afternoon block (3 hrs)
- [ ] **Mass balance** (1.5 hrs): Write out the general MBE from memory, derive all 4 special cases (steady/unsteady × conservative/non-conservative). Then do 2-3 CSTR problems (smokers-in-bar / wood-stove style) from the past papers until you can set one up without looking at notes.
- [ ] **Population projection** (45 min): Do the population table problem from 2 different past papers (arithmetic AND geometric method for each).
- [ ] **Water balance** (45 min): Do 3 evapotranspiration problems — they're nearly identical across papers, just different numbers.

### Evening block (2.5 hrs)
- [ ] **Solid waste numericals** (2 hrs): Practice the MC%/density table calc from at least 2 past papers. Then do 2 landfill-area problems and 1 Modified Dulong's Formula calc.
- [ ] Quick review of SWM process flow + waste hierarchy (30 min, recall only)

**End of Day 1 checkpoint:** You should be able to solve mass balance, population projection, water balance, and solid waste table problems without hesitation.

---

## Day 2 — Air Pollution, Water Treatment, Written-Answer Topics
**~6 hours**

### Morning block (2.5 hrs)
- [ ] **ESP + Deutsch-Anderson** (1 hr): Do 2 numericals (solve for area given η, and η given area) — these come from Ch.4 and appear in Section B almost every year.
- [ ] **Cyclone separators, sound dB combination** (30 min): Formula + 2-3 quick MCQs
- [ ] **Criteria air pollutants, AQI/AQS, Nepal standards** (1 hr): Recall pass — six pollutants, sources/effects, why winter pollution is worse (inversion layer)

### Afternoon block (2 hrs)
- [ ] **Water/wastewater treatment train** (1 hr): Screening → coagulation → sedimentation → filtration → disinfection, what each removes. Sewer hierarchy (house<lateral<main<outfall). BOD-mixing-at-a-point problem (1-2 practice reps).
- [ ] **LCA + Functional Unit + EIA/IEE/BES** (1 hr): Memorize the 4 LCA stages with one-line descriptions. Write out the Functional Unit definition + do 1 worked example (plates/markers style). Memorize 2-3 project-size thresholds for BES/IEE/EIA each.

### Evening block (1.5 hrs)
- [ ] **Green Engineering principles** (45 min): Pick your best 4 of the 12 principles, write a 2-sentence explanation + example for each, so you're covered no matter which 2 are asked.
- [ ] **Ch.1 topics** (45 min): IRR definition/significance, NEC objectives, traditional vs context-sensitive design, "lowest degree of freedom" concept (Incrementalism).

**End of Day 2 checkpoint:** All written-answer topics have a rehearsed 3-5 sentence answer ready. ESP and dB numericals feel routine.

---

## Day 3 — Full Drilling + Weak Spots (exam day or day before)
**~3.5–4 hours, front-loaded early**

### Block 1 (2 hrs)
- [ ] **MCQ speed run**: Go through Section A of all 6 past papers back to back, timed. Don't stop to re-derive — if you don't know it in 10 seconds, flag it and move on.
- [ ] Tally flagged questions — these are your actual weak spots, not what you assume they are.

### Block 2 (1.5 hrs)
- [ ] Fix weak spots from Block 1 directly — targeted, not general review.
- [ ] Do one full timed mock of a Section B from a paper you haven't touched yet (pick the one you're least familiar with) to check pacing under real conditions.

### Final pass (30 min, morning of exam if possible)
- [ ] Re-read your formula sheet once, top to bottom.
- [ ] Re-read your 4 rehearsed Green Engineering answers once.
- [ ] Do NOT start new material this close to the exam — diminishing returns, just raises anxiety.

---

## If You Only Have 2 Days (compression)

Merge Day 1 + Day 2 into one ~9-hour day (realistically split across the day with breaks), then Day 3 stays as the drilling day. Cut these first if squeezed:
- Ch.1 topics (IRR, NEC) — lowest mark density, easy to guess/partial-answer
- Cyclone separator detail — keep just enough to differentiate it from ESP
- Only memorize 2 Green Engineering principles deeply instead of 4

Do **not** cut: mass balance, population projection, solid waste numericals, water balance, ESP — these are the highest and most reliable mark sources across every single past paper.

---

## Notes
- Numerical practice > reading notes. Every past paper reuses near-identical problem shapes with different numbers — repetition is what makes them fast on exam day.
- Section A (MCQs) is worth doing last in bulk, since it's the fastest way to surface real gaps across the whole syllabus at once.
- Keep the formula sheet from the exam breakdown doc open/printed while practicing numericals — don't re-derive from scratch each time, just apply and speed up.
