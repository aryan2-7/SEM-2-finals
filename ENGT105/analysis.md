## 🔴 High Priority (appears constantly across papers + directly in your sample questions)

### 1. Document Design — the 4 aspects
Asked as a full 5-mark question in End-Sem 2025 and tested repeatedly in MCQs.
- **Organization** — logical structuring of content (headings, sections)
- **Order** — sequence in which information is presented
- **Access** — ease of finding information (TOC, indexing, headings, white space)
- **Variety** — visual variation (fonts, spacing, graphics) to maintain engagement

**Prep:** Memorize 2 concrete examples for each aspect. Also know: white space increases readability and separates sections (common MCQ).

### 2. Email / Memo / Letter Writing
A full subjective question in nearly every past paper:
- Intern progress email to a manager (your syllabus Sample Q2)
- Memo to HOD requesting materials / postponing a defense
- Formal letter (8 essential + 5 optional components — know these)
- Friend email about importance of presentation skills

**Standard structure:** Greeting → purpose/context → challenge or update → progress → request or ask → polite closing → signature

**cc vs bcc etiquette** — cc sends the same email to multiple visible recipients (recurring MCQ).

### 3. Résumé / Job Application
Full 5-mark question type (Data Analyst job application, Mining Engineer résumé).
Standard sections: Contact Info → Objective/Summary → Education → Skills → Experience → Certifications/Trainings

### 4. Audience Analysis
Recurring MCQ theme.
- **Lay audience** = little/no technical knowledge of the subject
- Contrast with expert and managerial audiences
- Key skill: explain concepts in simple, everyday language for lay readers

### 5. Digital Literacy vs. Social Media Use
Direct match to your syllabus Sample Q4.
- **Digital literacy** = ability to evaluate, create, and use digital information responsibly and critically
- **Social media use** = often passive consumption, not the same thing
- Answer structure: define both terms → argue they are NOT equivalent → give 2–3 reasons (e.g., literacy requires critical evaluation and creation skills that casual social media use doesn't develop)

## 🟡 Medium Priority

### 6. Internal Proposals & Reports
- Outline structure: Objectives, Project Description, Methodology, Timeline, Budget, Appendices
- **Appendices** = supporting materials, raw data, charts (NOT executive summaries or legal docs — common trap)
- **Executive Summary/Abstract** = 1–2 paragraph summary of findings (not methodology or budget)

### 7. Oral Presentation
- Grab attention at the start with a thought-provoking question or relevant story (NOT reading from script or listing points)
- Rehearsing multiple times → smooth delivery and familiarity with content
- Process flow: prewriting → drafting → rehearsal → delivery → post-presentation

### 8. Toulmin's Model of Argumentation
Appears heavily in MCQs even though borderline for unit scope — worth knowing:
- **Claim** — the main assertion
- **Warrant** — the logical link between claim and evidence
- **Backing** — support/justification for the warrant (e.g., "research from health organizations supports...")
- **Grounds/Data** — the evidence itself

### 9. Concept Paper Components
- Includes: Objectives, Methodology, Timeline, Budget Estimate, Research Problem/Question
- Does **NOT** typically include: detailed technical drawings (common "which is NOT included" MCQ trap)

## 🟢 Quick-Hit MCQ Concepts (Units I, III, IV relevant only)

Pulled from your MCQ collection, filtered to non-literature topics:

1. Prewriting stage = organizing ideas and planning structure (not editing/submitting)
2. Technical communication example = user manual (not novel/poem/essay)
3. "Accuracy" matters in tech comm because it ensures legal/safety compliance
4. Email insufficient when: delivering a legal notice or official document → use a letter instead
5. Instant messaging's primary benefit = real-time communication, quick responses
6. Headings/subheadings purpose = hierarchical structure + readability
7. Inductive reasoning = drawing a general conclusion from specific observations (vs. deductive = general → specific)
8. APA citation format basics (author, year, italicized title, publisher)
