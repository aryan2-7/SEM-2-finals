# Who are Audience?
The persons who reads your memo, blog entry,  web site, instant message, or reports that you write.
The group or individual who read your technical writing is your audience.
Audience recognition and involvement should be known for the effective communication.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Audience Recognition
When you are involved in any kind of technical communication (oral) the following questions should be considered.
Who is your audience?
What does the readers or listener know?
What does the readers or listener not know?
What must you write or say to ensure that your audience understands your points?
How do you communicate more that one person?
What is the person’s position in relation to your job title?
What diversity issues must you consider?

# Types of Audience

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# High Tech Audience
High tech work in your field of expertise.
Directly with you in your department or in a similar capacity for another company.
They are your colleague as they share your educational background, work experience or level of understanding.
Example: If your are computer engineer working in a company the person working as the computer engineer in another company is high tech audience.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Characteristics of High Tech Audience
Expert in the field you are writing about.
They share same level of understanding and academic qualification as you share.
They understand high tech jargons, acronyms, and abbreviations.
No need of explaining meaning of jargons.
They require minimal detail regarding standard procedures/scientific, mathematical or technical theories.
They  need little background information regarding  a project history or objectives if the subject matter is new to them.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Low-Tech Audience
Low tech audience are the co-workers who work with you in the other department of your company/organization.
Include you bosses, subordinate, or colleagues working for other companies too.
Your colleagues  who share same kinds of expertise also low tech if they don’t know your company’s jargons, acronyms and abbreviations.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Characteristics of Low Tech Audience
Familiar with the technology you are writing about but their job is peripheral to you as they might work in other department of your organization.
They understand the jargons used in your writing as they are familiar with the subject matter.
Technical concepts should be defined for low tech readers.
As low-tech reader is not someone to whom you write often regarding your field of expertise, provide more background information.
Low tech readers understand the basic concept of your work, but they not been involved in it daily.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Lay Audience  and /characteristics
Customers and clients who neither work for your company nor have any knowledge about your field of expertise.
Characteristics:
They are unfamiliar to your subject matter and terminology.
Topics should be clearly written using precise words, simple graphic.
They don’t understand your terminology or work environment. So avoid using in-house jargons and high tech terminology.
They need background information and clarity.

# Guidelines for effective Multicultural Communication
1. Define Acronyms and abbreviations
2. Avoid jargons and idioms
3. Distinguish between nouns and verbs.
4. Watch for Cultural biases/ Expectations
5. Be careful when using slash Marks
6. Avoid humor and puns
7. Realize that translation may take more or less space
8. consider Paper size
9. Avoid figurative language
10. Be careful with numbers, dates and tiems
11. Avoid biased language.
